VERDICT: ACCEPT WITH CORRECTIONS

The architecture (arrangement → flows → subsystems → contracts → settings → risk → derivation) is the right shape and is a genuine corrective to the rejected per-file census. But the outline documents one of the five trees it must describe. The scheduler, the bus, the fleet dispatcher and the inference proxy appear only as boxes in Part I and endpoints in Part IV — no chapters, no settings, no failure modes. If built as drawn, the handbook would document the node's toolbox and leave the control plane, the transport, the actual dispatch machinery and the credential boundary undocumented. A stranger still could not operate the system. Every correction below is mandatory, not advisory.

MERGE PROPOSAL: FAIL

Criterion: chapter-hood follows consequence, not size. A module earns a full chapter when it (a) is depended on by a majority of modules, (b) owns a primary flow's critical path, or (c) owns a hazard to state or evidence. M0 meets (a): twenty scripts source lib.sh and its fetch_verified (pinned + digest-checked) is the supply-chain root under every installer. M3 meets (b): land-node-branch.sh is F5, a primary flow, with a measured trap (a failed --ff-only once printed success). M8 meets (c): reclaim_disk has no age gate and can destroy cited evidence; check_scan_scope is the gitleaks gate. M7 owns the credential lifecycle this verdict adds as F12. Folding exactly these four into "Supporting subsystems" repeats the rejected census's mistake in a new shape — arranging by size instead of by consequence. The actual driver of the proposal is that these chapters would be short; the fix for that is the named-NONE rule (heading change 5), not the fold.
